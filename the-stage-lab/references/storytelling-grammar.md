# Stage Lab v2 — Storytelling Grammar (from master research, 2026-07-22)

The narrative and design rules every Stage Lab output follows. Distilled from Duarte (Resonate/sparkline), Presentation Zen, StudioBinder shot grammar, Pixar's 22 rules, Dan Harmon's Story Circle, McCloud's word–picture taxonomy, and pro graphic-recording practice (Ink Factory, ImageThink, Drawing Change).

## The 10 laws of stage visual storytelling

1. The audience is the hero; the presenter is the mentor; the villain is the audience's obstacle — never a person.
2. Alternate tension and release: What Is ↔ What Could Be, opened by a Call to Adventure, closed in New Bliss. A flat emotional line is a bug.
3. One idea, one image, one slide. Full-bleed, ≤5 words, graspable in 3 seconds from the back row.
4. Pictures beat words; pictures-plus-words beat both — only when interdependent. Never caption an image with what it already shows.
5. One STAR moment per talk (dramatization, sound bite, evocative visual, story, or shocking stat) — planned, rehearsed, singular.
6. Contrast is the engine at every scale: status-quo vs vision, focal point vs whitespace, warm vs cool.
7. Change the frame only when the story changed. New beat → new shot size/angle; same beat → hold.
8. Know the ending first: design the New Bliss image before the middle (Pixar rule 7).
9. Repetition is a feature of speech (repeat the key phrase 3–4×); clutter is a bug of slides (delete everything non-load-bearing).
10. Ration the strongest effects — motion, color, size, darkness: the more used, the less they mean.

## Sparkline rules (arc engine)

- 3 acts, 2 turning points: What Is → **Call to Adventure** (state the gap/Big Idea) → alternating What Is ↔ What Could Be → **Call to Action** (one concrete step) → **New Bliss** (end on the transformed future, not the homework).
- Each What-Is creates tension, each What-Could-Be releases it. Alternate analytical and emotional content (data ↔ anecdote).
- Words for pain, pictures for possibility. ~20% metaphorical language; anchor abstractions in shared concrete references; short poetic bursts.
- STAR moment: one only, balanced analytical/emotional, placed near the biggest turn.

## Beat → shot → light table (story-slides engine)

8-beat spine (Harmon Story Circle × Story Spine). 6-beat minimum: "Once upon a time / Every day / One day / Because of that / Because of that / Until finally."

| # | Beat | Function | Shot | Angle | Light / palette |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | You (Ordinary World) | comfort zone | wide, character small-but-at-home | eye level | high-key, warm, soft |
| 2 | Need (the Call) | disruption arrives | medium + insert on the disruptive thing | eye level, push-in | warmth draining, one cool accent |
| 3 | Go (Threshold) | commitment | full shot at a literal threshold | slightly low or high | warm behind, cool ahead |
| 4 | Search (Trials) | struggle, small wins | alternating MS/FS; two-shots for allies | mixed; dutch for setbacks | progressively lower-key |
| 5 | Find | gets what they wanted | medium close-up with the goal in frame | eye level, intimate | pool of light in dark (false dawn) |
| 6 | Take (Ordeal — LOW POINT) | price paid, all seems lost | CU/ECU (eyes, hands) or extreme wide, tiny and alone | high angle or overhead | lowest-key, coolest — chiaroscuro |
| 7 | Return (Resurrection) | transformed, final test | full shot in motion; OTS toward home | low angle (earned power) | light returning; backlit into dawn |
| 8 | Change (New Bliss) | same world, new person | MIRROR of beat 1 with visible differences, or CU of changed face | eye level | high-key, warmer/richer than beat 1 |

Constraints: beat 8 mirrors beat 1; STAR moment at beat 6 or the 6→7 turn; never solve beat 6 by coincidence (Pixar rule 19); open every new location wide; ≥3 different shot sizes per sequence; never two consecutive identical framings; stakes explicit (Pixar 16); admire characters for trying, not succeeding (Pixar 1).

Shot-size emotion map: EWS=insignificance/scale · WS=context · FS=action/body · MS=neutral/social · MCU=body language+face · CU=empathy/decision · ECU=maximum tension/one detail. Angles: low=power, high=vulnerability, eye=honesty, dutch=unease, overhead=fate. Light: high-key=openness/optimism, low-key single-source=tension/mystery, warm=comfort, cool=detachment, backlit silhouette=transformation.

## McCloud caption taxonomy (caption engine)

- **Picture-specific** (image carries it, ≤5-word caption): the default for flow slides.
- **Additive** (caption adds the "so what" the image can't show): for teaching slides.
- **Interdependent** (neither works alone): the gold standard — use on key beats.
- **Montage** (words drawn as part of the picture): graphic-recording mode, expressive lettering.
- **FORBIDDEN**: word-specific (bullet-slide failure) and duo-specific (caption repeats the image).

## Slide design rules (Presentation Zen engine)

- Billboard test: 3 seconds, back of the room. One focal point per slide via ONE contrast dimension (size, color, or position).
- Empty space is a feature. Rule of thirds: subject on a power point, never dead center (unless mirroring for effect).
- Dark venue → dark background/light text. Few colors, used semantically; warm advances, cool recedes.
- Chart titles are declarative sentences ("Cases dropped 17%"), Restrain–Reduce–Emphasize.
- Before/after comparisons side-by-side, both large. 2–3 transition types max per deck.

## Pro graphic-recording checklist (recap engine)

- Layout chosen BEFORE content (journey/river/radial/columns), paced so ~half the canvas fills by session midpoint.
- One dominant title + one large anchor image establishing the governing metaphor. Avoid icon-itis: occasional LARGE anchor illustrations beat tiny icons everywhere.
- Exactly 3 size tiers (title / key points / support). Key decisions = largest + most colorful.
- Black + max 2 colors (one warm, one cool); saturated color reserved for key points; identical color semantics across the whole canvas.
- Visible reading path (connectors trace the flow); hard containers = decided, open/cloud shapes = open questions.
- Synthesis, not transcription: distilled statements, decisions, tensions. Squint test: main points readable across the room.
