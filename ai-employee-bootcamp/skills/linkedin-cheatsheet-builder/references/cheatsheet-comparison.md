# Cheat Sheet Format · COMPARISON (table)
# by Daniel Paul · Purely Personal · distilled from Daniel's comparison cheatsheet prompt

Use when the router lands on: two things side by side. Three sub-formats. The router picks one; if the user only said "comparison", pick using the guide below.

---

## SUB-FORMAT SELECTION

**1 · X vs. Y Comparison (side-by-side analysis)**
Use when comparing two approaches, models, or strategies of equal weight. B2B vs B2C, Agency vs In-house, Old vs New Method. Tone: "Here are two distinct paths. Understand the real differences." Table: 6-8 category rows, 2 comparison columns.

**2 · Bad vs. Good Approach (right vs wrong)**
Use when showing tactical mistakes vs correct methods, organized by strategic dimensions. Tone: "Here's what's broken and here's what actually works." Table: 6-8 numbered rows, Bad and Good columns. (If the material is behaviors with 1:1 corrections rather than dimensions, that's Do's & Don'ts Side-by-Side, not this.)

**3 · What They Think vs. Reality (myth-busting)**
Use when debunking widespread misconceptions. Industry myths, belief shifts, counterintuitive truths. Tone: "Everyone believes this, but here's what's actually true." Table: 5-7 belief categories, Perception and Reality columns.

---

## SUB-FORMAT 1 · X VS. Y · STRICT STRUCTURE

```
Title: [Concept X] vs. [Concept Y] [optional descriptor]     (4-8 words)

| Category | [Concept X] | [Concept Y] |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| [Label 1] | □ point □ point □ point | □ point □ point □ point |
| [Label 2] | □ point □ point □ point | □ point □ point □ point |
[6-8 category rows]
| Similarities | [3-4 bullets spanning both, 10-14 words each] |
```

Rules:
- Title: clear comparison of two named concepts. "B2B vs. B2C Marketing", not "Two Different Approaches".
- Category labels: 1-3 words, each a different dimension, covering strategic, tactical, and operational. "Audience" / "Buying Cycles" / "Content Focus" / "Decision Drivers" / "Success Factors". Never "Information" or "Other Points".
- 2-4 points per cell (3 is ideal), 8-12 words each, clearly contrasting with the opposite column.
- Similarities row ONLY if both concepts share fundamental commonalities worth stating. Example: "Aim to attract, engage, and convert customers".
- Optional design notes after the table: icons per row, color coding per column.

## SUB-FORMAT 2 · BAD VS. GOOD · STRICT STRUCTURE

```
Title: Bad vs. Good [Specific Topic]     (5-8 words)

| # | Category | Bad [Topic] | Good [Topic] |
|---|----------|-------------|--------------|
| 1 | [Label] | [bad practice, 10-15 words] | [good practice, 10-15 words] |
[6-8 numbered rows]
| last | Keywords | [4-6 negative keywords as tags] | [4-6 positive keywords as tags] |
```

Rules:
- Category labels 1-3 words, end-to-end coverage: "Focus" / "Key Metric" / "Positioning" / "Messaging" / "Content" / "Mindset".
- Bad column: recognizable mistakes people actually make. Reader thinks "Oh no, I'm doing this". Example: "Seller-centric and focused on short term hacks for quick wins".
- Good column: the direct opposite or fix, actionable, aligned position-by-position with the bad column. Example: "Actively iterating based on customer feedback for long term revenue growth".
- Keywords row is the final row: keyword tags, not sentences. Bad: "Shiny Object Syndrome" | "Panic-led" | "Perfectionist". Good: "Focus" | "Discipline" | "Test & Learn" | "Audience-first".

## SUB-FORMAT 3 · THINK VS. REALITY · STRICT STRUCTURE

```
Title: What [Target Audience] Think vs. The Reality About [Topic]     (8-12 words, audience required)

| Category | ❌ What They Think | ✅ The Reality |
|----------|-------------------|----------------|
| [Label] | [common belief, 10-15 words] | [reality plus why it matters, 10-15 words] |
[5-7 category rows]

The Bottom Line: [one-sentence reality check, 14-18 words that challenges thinking]
```

Rules:
- Beliefs must be genuinely widespread and stated as confident declarations. NO straw men. "You need to post 3x per day to grow quickly" works. "Some people think trying helps" does not.
- Reality must counter AND educate: include the why or the impact, with evidence or numbers when the source material has them. "That's wrong" is not a reality.
- Bottom Line: memorable, quotable, names the shift in thinking.

---

## CATEGORY SELECTION GUIDE

Strategic comparisons (sub-formats 1 and 3): target audience, approach, key metrics, decision drivers, content strategy, timeline, positioning, success factors, tools, common challenges, similarities (1 only).
Tactical comparisons (sub-format 2): strategic focus, execution method, key metrics, positioning, messaging style, content approach, mindset, team structure, investment, keywords (final row).

Select 6-8 (5-7 for sub-format 3) that cover the topic comprehensively, flow strategic to tactical, answer the reader's key questions, and exist in the source material.

---

## WRITING STANDARDS (this format)

- Category labels 1-3 words strict. Points 8-15 words, shorter (6-8) for impact, longer (15-18) only when evidence demands.
- Left vs right must be clearly contrasting, never subtle. Each row stands alone as a complete comparison.
- No repeating the same idea across rows in different words. Numbers and percentages whenever the source has them.
- Use □ or • for multi-point cells, ❌ ✅ markers in sub-format 3, bold category labels and headers.
- Forbidden: obvious comparisons ("Bad: do nothing | Good: do something"), false or subtle contrasts, beliefs nobody holds, realities without impact, oversimplification that loses accuracy.

---

## WORKFLOW

1. **Understand the comparison.** What two things, or which belief vs which reality? Is this comparison genuinely needed?
2. **Select categories.** List every dimension in the source, pick 6-8 (5-7 for Think vs Reality), order strategic to tactical.
3. **Extract comparison points.** Per category, from source material only. Verify genuine contrast and note stats.
4. **Craft the title.** Specific, hooky, audience named for sub-format 3.
5. **Build the table.** Labels left, 1-4 points per cell, voice applied, technical terms verbatim.
6. **Add the special element.** Similarities (1), Keywords tags (2), or Bottom Line (3). Optional design notes for the render.
7. **Verify.** Every row genuinely contrasts, terminology exact, banned-word scan, left side authentically common, right side clear value with impact.

## WORKED EXAMPLE (few-shot calibration · coach/consultant topic · sub-format 3 · intent: NURTURING)

Title: What Coaches Think vs. The Reality About Getting Clients on LinkedIn

| Category | ❌ What They Think | ✅ The Reality |
|----------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Posting Volume** | You need to post every single day or the algorithm buries you | Three specific posts weekly with real comments outperform daily generic content for leads |
| **Follower Count** | You need at least 10,000 followers before clients start reaching out | Coaches with 800 engaged ICP followers book more calls than broad 10K accounts |
| **Content Type** | Motivational quotes and industry news build authority with potential clients | Client stories with real numbers get DMs. Inspiration only gets likes |
| **Sales Process** | Clients see your posts, decide alone, and message you ready to buy | Most clients need one DM conversation and one call. Posts open doors, conversations close |
| **Timeline** | One viral post can bring a full pipeline of clients overnight | Consistent posting takes 60 to 90 days to produce steady inbound conversations |
| **Profile** | The content matters most, the profile is just a formality | Half your post viewers click your profile. A weak headline loses them there |

The Bottom Line: Stop performing for the algorithm and start building proof for one person. Leads follow proof, not volume.

Why this passes: audience named in the title (coaches), six distinct dimensions ordered strategy to mechanics, every belief is one coaches actually voice, every reality includes the impact and a number where one exists, Bottom Line is 17 words and quotable.
