# Cheat Sheet Format · DO'S & DON'TS (multi-structure)
# by Daniel Paul · Purely Personal · distilled from Daniel's dos and don'ts cheatsheet prompt

Use when the router lands on: right vs wrong BEHAVIORS on one topic. Three sub-formats. The router picks one; confirm with the decision tree below when the material could go either way.

---

## SUB-FORMAT DECISION TREE

Ask in order:
1. Do the practices naturally pair as direct opposites? YES → Side-by-Side Pairs.
2. Is there a clear priority or severity hierarchy among mistakes? YES → Numbered Countdown.
3. Primary goal? Comprehensive coverage → Classic Sections. Direct correction → Side-by-Side. Urgency and prioritization → Countdown.
4. Rotation: never the same sub-format 3 times in a row.
5. What does the source suggest? Ranked mistakes → Countdown. Clear before/after → Side-by-Side. Multiple angles → Classic.

---

## SUB-FORMAT 1 · CLASSIC SEPARATE SECTIONS

```
[COMPELLING TITLE]: [Topic] Dos and Don'ts     (8-12 words)

❌ DON'T:
- [Wrong practice 1]
  → Why it fails: [specific consequence, 10-15 words]
[5-7 don'ts total]

✅ DO:
- [Right practice 1]
  → Why it works: [specific benefit, 10-15 words]
[5-7 dos total]

💡 The Bottom Line: [key takeaway, 12-18 words]
```

Rules:
- Title format "[Compelling phrase]: [Topic] Dos and Don'ts". "Stop Guessing: KPI Tracking Dos and Don'ts" works. "Business Tips" does not.
- DON'T count and DO count need not match, and practices need NOT pair 1:1. Each section stands alone.
- Use when: multiple unrelated practices, comprehensive coverage, breadth of knowledge.

## SUB-FORMAT 2 · SIDE-BY-SIDE PAIRS (direct opposites)

```
[COMPELLING TITLE]: [Topic] - What to Stop vs. What to Start     (8-12 words)

| # | ❌ STOP DOING THIS | ✅ START DOING THIS |
|---|--------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | [wrong practice, 8-12 words] → Why it fails: [consequence, 10-15 words] | [right practice, 8-12 words] → Why it works: [benefit, 10-15 words] |
[exactly 5-6 paired rows]

💡 The Shift: [the mindset change needed, 12-18 words]
```

Rules:
- Title signals direct comparison: "[Topic] - What to Stop vs. What to Start" or "[Topic]: Wrong Way vs. Right Way".
- Every STOP has one corresponding START: same concept, opposite execution. "Track 20 metrics monthly" → "Track 3 metrics daily" pairs. "Track too many things" → "Build better systems" does not.
- The Shift closes on the mindset change: "Move from measuring everything to mastering what matters. Precision beats volume."
- Use when: each wrong practice has a clear right alternative, showing before/after, maximum contrast impact.

## SUB-FORMAT 3 · NUMBERED COUNTDOWN (priority-based table)

```
[NUMBER] [Topic] Mistakes Killing Your [Outcome] (Ranked)     (8-12 words, number required)

| # | The Mistake | Why It's Not Working | What to Do Instead | The Result |
|---|-------------|----------------------|--------------------|------------|
| 5 | [8-12 words] | [consequence, 10-15 words] | [correction, 10-15 words] | [benefit when fixed, 10-15 words] |
| 4 | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 3 | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 2 | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 1 | [THE BIGGEST mistake] | ... | ... | ... |

🎯 The Wake-Up Call: [urgent final message, 12-18 words]
```

Rules:
- Exactly 5 mistakes (6 only if the content strongly demands), numbered in REVERSE: #5 least critical, #1 most critical. The countdown builds suspense and priority clarity.
- Ranking logic must be defensible, by one of: impact severity, commonality plus impact, foundation first, or urgency.
- The Wake-Up Call creates urgency, usually pointing at #1: "Fix mistake #1 first. It's costing you more than all others combined."
- Use when: clear priority hierarchy, urgency framing, guiding the reader on "fix this first".

---

## WRITING STANDARDS (this format)

Word counts: practices and mistakes 8-12 words · consequences and benefits 10-15 words · closing statement 12-18 words. Slight variation allowed for natural flow, never at the cost of clarity.

**DON'Ts / STOPs / Mistakes:** common errors people actually make, specific about the wrong action. Reader reaction: "Oh no, I do this." Example: "Track 20 different metrics and review them only once per month".

**DOs / STARTs / Corrections:** implementable today, specific, the clear better alternative. Example: "Pick three core metrics and review them with your team daily".

**Consequences ("Why it fails"):** real cost in time, money, opportunity, or credibility. Tangible. Example: "Teams lose focus across too many numbers and improve nothing consistently". Never "it doesn't work well".

**Benefits ("Why it works" / "The Result"):** real gain, quantified when the source allows. Example: "Everyone knows exactly what success means and can adjust behavior same day". Never "better outcomes".

Forbidden: vague practices ("don't make mistakes"), generic consequences ("you'll fail"), obvious advice ("don't ignore customers"), benefits without specificity, straw-man mistakes nobody makes, practices that aren't actionable.

---

## WORKFLOW

1. **Analyze content type.** List every mistake and every right practice in the source. Check for hierarchy and for 1:1 pairing.
2. **Select sub-format.** Decision tree above, plus rotation.
3. **Structure.** Classic: two stand-alone lists. Pairs: 5-6 direct opposites, aligned. Countdown: rank #5 to #1 with defensible logic.
4. **Craft the title** to the sub-format's pattern. Curiosity or urgency required.
5. **Write practices and explanations** to the word counts, voice applied, technical terms verbatim.
6. **Write the closing statement.** Bottom Line (1), The Shift (2), or Wake-Up Call (3). 12-18 words, memorable.
7. **Verify.** Structure followed precisely, every practice specific and recognizable, every consequence tangible, every benefit motivating, banned-word scan, read-aloud test.

## WORKED EXAMPLE (few-shot calibration · coach/consultant topic · sub-format 3 · intent: SOFT-SELL)

5 Discovery Call Mistakes Killing Your Close Rate (Ranked)

| # | The Mistake | Why It's Not Working | What to Do Instead | The Result |
|---|-------------|----------------------|--------------------|------------|
| 5 | Sending a generic calendar link with no prep questions attached | Prospects arrive cold and spend twenty minutes explaining basics you could know | Add three intake questions to the booking form about goal and blocker | Calls start at the real problem and feel like coaching, not intake |
| 4 | Opening the call with your background, method, and credentials | Prospects tune out because the first ten minutes are about you, not them | Open with one question: what made you book this call today | Prospect talks first, names the stakes, and sells themselves on urgency |
| 3 | Coaching for free for forty minutes to prove your value | The prospect leaves satisfied, solved enough for now, and never buys | Diagnose the problem fully but save the treatment plan for paid work | Prospect sees the gap clearly and needs you to close it |
| 2 | Quoting your price and immediately explaining or discounting it | Justifying the number signals doubt and invites negotiation on every call | State the price, then stop talking until the prospect responds | Silence does the selling. Objections surface honestly instead of politely later |
| 1 | Ending with "think it over and let me know" | No next step means the decision dies in their inbox within days | Book the decision call before hanging up, with a clear yes-or-no date | Close rate doubles because every call ends with a scheduled decision |

🎯 The Wake-Up Call: Fix mistake #1 first. Every open loop you leave costs you a client someone else closes.

Why this passes: five mistakes ranked by revenue impact with #1 as the deal killer, every mistake is one coaches recognize from their own calls, every correction is doable on the next call, every result names the payoff, the countdown builds to the close, and the whole table quietly argues for a better sales process without naming an offer (soft-sell intent).
