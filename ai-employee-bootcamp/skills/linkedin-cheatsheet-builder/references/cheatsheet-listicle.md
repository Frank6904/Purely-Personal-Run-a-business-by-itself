# Cheat Sheet Format · LISTICLE (6 main points x 4 takeaways)
# by Daniel Paul · Purely Personal · distilled from Daniel's cheatsheet listicle prompt

Use when the router lands on: one topic that breaks into steps, phases, principles, or a checklist. The default format for EDUCATING intent and the progression-framework home for SOFT-SELL and HARD-SELL.

---

## STRICT OUTPUT STRUCTURE (design-ready, no deviations)

```
Head: [8-10 words with niche-specific hook and pain point]
Subheading: [10-12 words explaining why this matters now]

Main Point 1: [headline, 6-8 words]
→ [takeaway, 10-12 words, actionable and specific]
→ [takeaway, 10-12 words, actionable and specific]
→ [takeaway, 10-12 words, actionable and specific]
→ [takeaway, 10-12 words, actionable and specific]

[continue for 6 main points total]
```

No "Key takeaway:" labels. No extra commentary. The design (or the HTML render) handles visual treatment.

---

## HEAD (8-10 words max)

Must include all three:
1. Niche-specific context: name the industry, role, or problem space
2. A clear pain point or promise
3. An attention grabber: number, bold claim, or curiosity hook

Formula patterns that work:
- "The [number] [niche-specific thing] That [result/problem]"
- "[Niche] [Problem]: Why [common belief] Is Wrong"
- "How [niche professionals] [achieve outcome] Without [pain point]"

Good: "OTA Visa Profit Center Checklist 2025" · "The Integration Trap That Silently Kills MVP Timelines"
Bad: "Important Things About Business" · "Six Steps to Success"

Never use generic terms when niche terms exist:
"Business owners" → "OTA founders" or "SaaS CEOs"
"Better results" → "85% digital attachment rate"

## SUBHEADING (10-12 words max)

Answers: "Why should I care about this RIGHT NOW?" It elaborates the pain, promises the transformation, adds proof, or creates urgency.

Formula patterns:
- "Stop [painful current state] and start [desired state]. Here's how."
- "[Specific outcome] for [niche] without [common obstacle]."
- "The [framework/system] that [specific transformation]."

Head plus subheading work as a micro-pitch. Together the reader thinks: "This is FOR ME and I need this NOW."

## PROGRESSION FRAMEWORKS BY INTENT

All 6 points follow ONE logical path. Never randomly select points from different topics. Pick the framework that matches the intent chosen in Step 1.

**EDUCATING · Framework A: Foundation then Layers**
1 Core concept and why it matters · 2 First principle · 3 Second principle · 4 Third principle · 5 Application method · 6 Common mistakes to avoid

**EDUCATING · Framework B: Sequential Process**
1 Phase 1 (Foundation) · 2 Phase 2 (Discovery) · 3 Phase 3 (Testing) · 4 Phase 4 (Refinement) · 5 Phase 5 (Mastery) · 6 Integration and next steps

**NURTURING · Problem, Insight, Truth**
1 The surface problem everyone sees · 2 The hidden root cause they miss · 3 Why conventional solutions fail · 4 The counterintuitive truth · 5 What experts do differently · 6 How to start thinking this way

**SOFT-SELL · Pain, Gap, Need**
1 Current broken process · 2 Why it's worse than they think · 3 Cost of inaction (time, money, risk) · 4 What's possible instead · 5 Requirements for transformation · 6 First steps toward the solution

**HARD-SELL · Feature, Benefit, Proof**
1 Core offering overview · 2 Key benefit #1 · 3 Key benefit #2 · 4 Key benefit #3 · 5 Results, proof, ROI · 6 How to get started (CTA)

**ENGAGEMENT · Provocation then Reflection**
1 Controversial or surprising truth · 2 Common mistake #1 and why · 3 Common mistake #2 and why · 4 Self-assessment question · 5 Alternative approach · 6 Discussion prompt or challenge

**Coherence check before finalizing.** Read points 1 to 6 in sequence:
- Does each point naturally lead to the next?
- Could you explain the connection between any two adjacent points?
- Do all points serve the same intent?
- Is there a clear beginning, middle, and end?
- Would removing any point break the flow?
Any "no" means restructure.

## MAIN POINT HEADLINES (6-8 words, strict)

Frame as outcome, insight, or action. Never a generic category.
Good: "Pricing Structure for Digital Travel Platforms" · "Authentication Differences Compound Quickly" · "The Clarity Phase"
Bad: "Important Tips" · "Things to Know"

## TAKEAWAYS (4 per point · 10-12 words, flexible 9-14)

Start with "→". Each one IMMEDIATELY ACTIONABLE. Each passes the "So what?" test: WHAT to do, HOW or WHEN to do it, specific enough to act on today.

Good:
→ "Create 3-tier visa service structure (Basic, Premium, and VIP)"
→ "Assume every third-party service adds logic, failure modes, testing complexity"
→ "Add at least forty percent buffer time for multi-service integrations"

Bad:
→ "Communication is important" (not actionable)
→ "Focus on quality" (vague)

Forbidden in any takeaway: motivational statements, obvious advice everyone knows, vague instructions, restating the main point, corporate jargon without context, information without application.

## WORKFLOW

1. **Understand intent.** Read the brain dump and source. Confirm the intent chosen in Step 1 matches the business goal.
2. **Extract.** List all main concepts, the participant's technical terms, their frameworks, and every stat or proof point. Source material only. Nothing invented.
3. **Determine flow.** Pick the progression framework for the intent. Map 6 points that build. Test each connection.
4. **Craft head and subheading.** Niche pain or promise first, then the words. Test the micro-pitch.
5. **Write points and takeaways.** Headlines 6-8 words. 4 takeaways each, 10-12 words, all actionable, technical terms verbatim, voice applied.
6. **Verify.** Intent alignment, logical progression, actionability of every takeaway, terminology accuracy, banned-word scan, "sounds like the participant" read-aloud test.

## WORKED EXAMPLE (few-shot calibration · coach/consultant topic · intent: EDUCATING · Framework B)

Head: The 6-Step System Coaches Use to Turn Posts Into Clients
Subheading: Stop posting into the void and start booking discovery calls weekly.

Main Point 1: Define One Reader Before You Write
→ Name one real person your last three clients have in common
→ Write their exact words for the problem, not your expert language
→ Keep a running note of questions they ask on calls
→ Test every draft against them: would this person stop scrolling

Main Point 2: Fix the Profile Before the Content
→ Rewrite your headline as outcome plus proof, not job title
→ Pin one post that shows a named client result with numbers
→ Make your About section answer one question: what changes for me
→ End the About section with a keyword to DM you

Main Point 3: Post Three Times Weekly, Not Daily
→ Three specific posts beat seven generic ones for inbound leads
→ Batch write on one morning so posting never blocks client work
→ Rotate one story, one framework, one opinion post each week
→ Repost your best performer after eight weeks with a new hook

Main Point 4: Spend 20 Minutes Daily in Comments
→ Comment on ten ICP posts before you publish your own
→ Add a view from client work, never "great post" filler
→ Reply to every comment on your post within two hours
→ Track which commenters match your ICP in a simple list

Main Point 5: Move Warm Conversations Off the Feed
→ DM every new follower who fits the ICP within two days
→ Open with their content or comment, never with your pitch
→ Ask one question about their situation before offering anything
→ Offer the call only after they name the problem themselves

Main Point 6: Track Weekly Leads, Not Post Likes
→ Count DMs started, calls booked, and proposals sent every Friday
→ Ignore impressions unless they correlate with conversations in your tracker
→ Kill any post type that gets likes but never leads
→ Double down on the one format that booked last month's calls

Why this passes: one intent (educating), sequential progression (reader, profile, content, engagement, conversion, measurement), every takeaway names a number or a concrete action, head names the niche (coaches) plus the promise (posts into clients), subheading adds urgency (weekly calls).
