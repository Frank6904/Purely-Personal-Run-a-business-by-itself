## 1. COVER PATTERNS BY INTENT

The cover title is 4 to 8 words. The subtitle is 8 to 15 words. Shorter is stronger.

### Educating
- "The [Number]-Step Process to [Specific Outcome]"
- "[Number] [Things] That [Achieve Result]"
- Example: "The 6-Step Process to Make AI Sound Exactly Like You"

### Nurturing
- "I [Did X] in [Timeframe] (Here's How)"
- "I Built [X] Without [Common Requirement]"
- Example: "I Trained Claude to Write Like Me (5+ Calls in 1 Week)"

### Soft Selling
- "[Client/Result] Got [Outcome] Using This System"
- "[Tool/Method] Just [Did X]. Here's the Process."
- Example: "My Client Got 5 ICP Leads Using This AI System"

### Hard Selling
- "Want [Outcome] in [Timeframe]?"
- "[Offer]: [Number] Spots Opening [When]"
- Example: "Want Voice-Trained AI That Converts?"

### Engagement
- "I [Do Y] Instead of [Popular Thing]"
- "Stop [Common Action]. Do [Better Action] Instead."
- Example: "I Stopped Writing LinkedIn Posts (Here's What Happened)"

### General-purpose title formulas (any intent, proven high-performers)
1. Personal achievement + time: "I [Did X] in [Y Minutes/Hours] (Here's How)"
2. Outcome without expected input: "[Achievement] Without [Common Barrier]"
3. Contrarian claim: "I [Do Y] Instead of [Popular Thing]"
4. Tool + specific outcome: "Claude Trained on My Voice Booked 5 ICP Calls in 1 Week"
5. Number + promise: "The [Number]-Step Process to [Specific Outcome]"

## 2. SUBTITLE FORMULAS

1. Method clarification: "[Action/Method], not [Alternative]"
   Example: "Train AI on your voice, not generic prompts everyone uses"
2. Outcome expansion: "[Achieve X] without [Pain Point]"
   Example: "Get ICP leads DMing you without writing every post yourself"
3. Process preview: "The exact [system] I use to [outcome]"
   Example: "The exact 6-step process that got our client 5 inbound leads"
4. Target + promise: "For [Audience] who want [Outcome]"
   Example: "For founders who want AI content that actually converts"

---

## 3. COVER PRINCIPLES AND FORBIDDEN PATTERNS

Principles:
- Specific over vague: "5+ calls in 1 week" beats "more leads".
- Outcome-focused: what they achieve, not what they learn.
- Conversational: "I Trained Claude" beats "How to Use AI".
- Curiosity gap: the reader must NEED to know how.
- Intent-aligned: cover promise matches the chosen intent's CTA.
- Visual works at thumbnail size: outcome stat, before/after split, bold claim.

Forbidden on covers:
- "Steal My Exact Process!!" (overused, spammy)
- Multiple exclamation marks
- "The Ultimate Guide to [X]"
- "Everything You Need to Know About [X]"
- "The Secret to [X]"
- Generic buzzwords without specificity

## 4. FLOW PATTERNS

Match the pattern to the reference material, then map it onto the chosen slide count.

### Pattern A: Problem → Solution Framework
Cover: hook with a surprising truth or bold claim
Slide 2: problem identification (the pain)
Slides 3-4: why current approaches fail
Middle slides: solution steps or framework
Second-to-last: proof or results (if real proof exists)
Last: CTA slide

### Pattern B: Transformation Journey
Cover: before state (the pain point)
Slide 2: what's possible (the after state)
Middle slides: steps to get there
Second-to-last group: common pitfalls, then results
Last: CTA slide

### Pattern C: How-To / Tutorial
Cover: outcome achieved + time or ease claim
Slide 2: why this matters, the problem it solves
Middle slides: step-by-step process, one step per slide
Second-to-last: why this works, results
Last: CTA slide

### Pattern D: Framework / System
Cover: the problem this solves
Slide 2: why existing solutions fail
Slide 3: framework introduction (name it)
Middle slides: each framework component, one per slide
Second-to-last: how it works together
Last: CTA slide

### Intent-to-flow mapping
- Educating: Problem → Steps (one per slide) → Why it works (Pattern C or D)
- Nurturing: Before state → Journey → Lessons → Application (Pattern B)
- Soft Selling: Challenge → Failed approaches → System → Results (Pattern A)
- Hard Selling: Problem → Benefits → Proof → Urgency (Pattern A, compressed)
- Engagement: Wrong belief → Truth → Proof → Meaning (Pattern A, contrarian cover)

### Flow connections between slides
- Bridging questions: "So what does this mean?" → next slide answers.
- Consequence connections: "When X happens..." → next slide shows the result.
- Building patterns: "First..." → "Then..." → "Finally..."
- The client's own transition phrases from their Voice DNA.

## 5. CTA PATTERNS BY INTENT

One clear action per CTA slide. Never stack follow AND comment AND DM.

### Type 1: FOLLOW (Educating and Nurturing)
Titles: "Want More [Content Type] Like This?" · "Follow for More [Topic] Systems"
Content formula: "Follow me for [specific value]. I share [content type] that [outcome], not [what you don't do]."
Example:
- Title: Want More AI Systems Like This?
- Content: Follow for frameworks that turn AI into your content team. I share what works for founders. No fluff, just systems that book calls.
- Visual: profile photo + "Follow" button

### Type 2: COMMENT KEYWORD (Soft and Hard Selling)
Titles: "Want [Outcome] in [Timeframe]?" · "Ready to [Achieve X]?" · "Want This System Built For You?"
Content formula: "Comment '[KEYWORD]' below and I'll [specific action]. [Genuine scarcity only]."
Example:
- Title: Want This System Built For You?
- Content: Comment "AI" below. Opening 5 spots this month for founders who want voice-trained AI systems. I'll send details.
- Visual: bold "Comment AI" with arrow to the comment section

### Type 3: DM KEYWORD (Hard Selling, premium offers)
Titles: "Ready to [Transformation]?" · "[Number] Spots Available This [Timeframe]"
Content formula: "DM me '[KEYWORD]' to [get thing]. [Qualification or scarcity]."
Example:
- Title: 5 Spots Available This Month
- Content: DM me "VOICE" to get the application. For founders ready to invest $5K+ in their content system.
- Visual: DM icon + "Send VOICE"

### Type 4: FOLLOW + NICHE (Engagement)
Titles: "Follow for More [Industry] Content" · "[Industry] Founders: More Systems Coming"
Content formula: "I share [content type] specifically for [industry]. Follow if you want [outcome] for your [industry] business."
Example:
- Title: Follow for More SaaS Growth Systems
- Content: I share AI systems specifically for SaaS founders. Follow if you want inbound leads without hiring a content team.
- Visual: profile photo + industry tagline

### Type 5: PROOF + CTA (case-study carousels, either selling intent)
Titles: "This Works. Here's How to Start." · "[Result] Is Possible. Here's Your First Step."
Content formula: "[Client/I] achieved [result] using this exact system. Want the same? [One action]."
Example:
- Title: 5 ICP Leads in 3 Weeks. Your Turn.
- Content: Our client followed this exact process and got 5 ICP leads DMing them. Comment "VOICE" if you want the setup.
- Visual: DM screenshots + "Comment VOICE"

### CTA principles and forbidden patterns
Principles: one action · specific outcome named · intent-aligned · genuine scarcity only · visual supports the action.
Forbidden: "Like, comment, and follow!" · "Don't miss this, share it!" · fake urgency · generic "Follow for more content" · long paragraphs explaining the offer · multiple exclamation marks.

## 6. WRITING STANDARDS FOR SLIDES

- Vocabulary: grade 3 to 4 reading level, except technical terms the client uses.
- Sentences: 10 to 15 words primary range, 6 to 10 for impact, 16 to 18 only when flow demands.
- Never sacrifice natural flow to hit a word count. Vary rhythm.
- Content sources: ONLY the reference context, the brain, and the user's answers.
  No invented facts, stats, client names, or examples.
- Technical terms stay exactly as the client uses them: "Voice DNA", "ICP", "Claude Projects",
  "EMR integration", "KPI dashboard". Their audience expects precise language.
  Dumbing it down reduces credibility.
- Forbidden: every headline starting "How to" · motivational fluff · slides repeating one
  point in new words · corporate presentation language · bullet fragments instead of
  sentences · information dumps without insight.
- The full AI blacklist in human-writing-standards.md applies to every slide and the caption.

## 7. GOOD SLIDE vs BAD SLIDE

**Bad body slide (three violations):**
```
Slide 5
Headline: The Power of Consistent Content Strategies for Growth
Support:  Consistency is key when it comes to building your personal brand.
          You need to show up every day and provide value to your audience.
          This will help you grow and reach your goals over time.
Visual:   graph
```
Why it fails: corporate headline built on the forbidden "The Power of" pattern,
zero specifics, three lines that all say "post often", visual idea says nothing.

**Good body slide (same topic):**
```
Slide 5
Headline: Two posts beat twenty-two
Support:  In January I posted twice. Both written for one exact reader.
          That month out-earned my 22-post month by 3x.
Visual:   side-by-side bar chart: "22 posts / £4K" vs "2 posts / £12K"
```
Why it works: 4-word headline with a curiosity gap, one idea (targeting beats volume),
real numbers, a visual that proves the claim at a glance.

## 8. WORKED EXAMPLE · COMPLETE 10-SLIDE CAROUSEL

Intent: Soft Selling · Flow: Pattern A (Challenge → Failed approaches → System → Results)
Reader: coaches with an engaged audience and no inbound leads.
All numbers below came from intake. Never reuse them for another client.

**COVER**
Title: My Client Booked 7 Calls in 3 Weeks
Subtitle: The LinkedIn system that turned a silent audience into discovery calls
Visual: black background, giant "0 → 7" in red, "discovery calls, 21 days" beneath

**SLIDE 2**
Headline: 8 months of posting. Zero inbound.
Support: Sarah is a leadership coach with 4,000 followers.
Solid likes on every post. Not one discovery call booked.
Visual: flat line graph labeled "inbound calls, Jan to Aug"

**SLIDE 3**
Headline: She tried everything the gurus teach
Support: Daily posting. Trending hooks. An engagement pod that felt like a part-time job.
More reach every month. Still nothing in the calendar.
Visual: checklist with every item ticked, calendar beside it empty

**SLIDE 4**
Headline: The real problem wasn't effort
Support: Her content proved she was smart.
It never showed what working with her actually changes.
Visual: split card: "Impressive" vs "Hireable"

**SLIDE 5**
Headline: Step 1: Write for one reader
Support: We rewrote every post for one person. The newly promoted director drowning in back-to-back 1:1s.
Everyone else was allowed to scroll past.
Visual: crowd silhouette with a single figure highlighted in red

**SLIDE 6**
Headline: Step 2: Swap frameworks for receipts
Support: Two of her three weekly posts became client stories with numbers.
"Maya cut her meeting load 40% in 6 weeks" beats any model.
Visual: post mockup showing a before/after stat

**SLIDE 7**
Headline: Step 3: One keyword, once a week
Support: Every Thursday, one post ended with a single ask.
"Comment CALM and I'll send the delegation scorecard."
Visual: comment thread mockup filling with "CALM"

**SLIDE 8**
Headline: Day 21: seven discovery calls booked
Support: Three came from one client-story post.
Two of the seven became clients within the month.
Visual: calendar screenshot concept, seven slots highlighted in red

**SLIDE 9**
Headline: Why this works: specificity filters
Support: Vague content collects likes from everyone and calls from no one.
Specific content makes the wrong people leave and the right ones feel found.
Visual: funnel: "4,000 followers → 1 exact reader → 7 calls"

**SLIDE 10 (CTA)**
Title: Want this system in your business?
Content: Comment "SYSTEM" below and I'll send the exact 3-step breakdown Sarah used.
Visual: bold "Comment SYSTEM" with arrow to the comment section

**CAPTION (623 characters)**
7 discovery calls in 3 weeks. From an audience that ignored her for 8 months.

Sarah did everything right on paper. Posted daily, studied hooks, kept her engagement
climbing. The likes came. The calls never did.

We changed three things. None of them involved posting more. One of them will feel
completely wrong to most coaches, and it's the one that filled her calendar.

The full breakdown is in the slides. Slide 9 is the one most people skip, and it's the
reason the other eight work.

Comment SYSTEM and I'll send you the 3-step breakdown.

P.S. Her third post using this got more calls than her previous 90 combined.

**METADATA**
Intent: Soft Selling · Slides: 10 · Score: 4/4
Specificity: "Maya cut her meeting load 40% in 6 weeks" · Voice: "Two posts beat twenty-two" rhythm, no opener starting with "I" · Stakes: "The likes came. The calls never did." · Surprise: "Everyone else was allowed to scroll past."
